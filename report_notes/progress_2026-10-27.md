## Implementation decisions (mention in the report)

- **All 7 pairs kept.** Matching is always MT vs reference in the *same* language, so per-language
  monolingual fastText vectors (`cc.en/cs/de/ru/zh.300`) suffice; cross-lingual alignment is not needed.
  Only the 400k most frequent words per language are read.
- **P(w) for SIF weights** comes from Zipf's law on the fastText frequency rank: P = 1 / (rank · H_N), N = 2M.
- **Unknown words** get a deterministic random unit vector (seeded by the word's MD5): they match
  themselves exactly and are near-orthogonal to everything else.
- **Order-invariance of eq. (4).** Summed word vectors ignore word order, so "12 jeho" and "jeho 12" have
  cosine 1. Non-identical n-grams are capped at cosine 0.99999 so that δ = 1 still reproduces BLEU exactly
  (verified to within 1e-13 against sacrebleu). For δ < 1, reordered n-grams still get almost full credit.
- **Exact matches are never lost by LSH:** n-gram vectors are summed so that identical n-grams get
  bit-identical vectors, hence identical hash keys.
- **Smoothing** mirrors sacrebleu sentence BLEU (exp smoothing, effective order, score 0 if nothing matches).
- **Hyperplanes** are drawn once (seed 42) as 32 tables × 16 bits; configuration (k, L) uses the first
  L tables and first k bits, so all grid points come from the same random draw.

## WMT19 dev results

Average over the 7 pairs:

| Metric | Pearson ρ | Kendall τ | Similarity computations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sentence BLEU | 0.299 | 0.207 | — |
| chrF | 0.371 | 0.244 | — |
| Soft matching, exact all-pairs (δ = 0.6) | 0.371 | 0.243 | 11,590,298 (100%) |
| **LSH-eBLEU (δ = 0.6, k = 10, L = 32)** | **0.370** | **0.243** | **2,273,082 (19.6%)** |

- δ chosen by best average Pearson (sweep 0.5–1.0, `results/dev_delta_sweep.csv`). Correlation is flat for
  0.5–0.65 and drops steadily toward δ = 1 (= BLEU).
- (k, L) rule: fewest similarity computations among settings within 0.005 of exact soft matching on both
  ρ and τ (`results/dev_lsh_grid.csv`, 42 settings).
- Measured collision rates match eq. (10) (e.g. k = 8, L = 16: cos 0.6–0.8 → 0.78 measured vs 0.74 theory).

### Points to be honest about in the report

1. **Soft matching beats BLEU on all 7 pairs (+0.07 ρ on average) but only ties chrF on average.** Against
   chrF it wins on en-cs (0.391 vs 0.366) and en-zh (0.291 vs 0.240) and is slightly behind on the other five
   (largest gap ru-en: 0.286 vs 0.325).
2. **LSH cuts similarity computations by 5×, but hashing itself costs more.** Each n-gram needs k·L = 320
   random projections, i.e. 232.8M projection dot products vs 11.6M for exact all-pairs. With ~20-token
   references, brute force is cheaper in total. LSH should only pay off when the reference side is large
   (a whole document, many references, or a reference indexed once and reused). A scaling experiment
   showing that crossover would be a good addition.
